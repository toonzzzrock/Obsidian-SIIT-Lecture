### Comprehensive Lecture Summary

#### 1. The Epistemological Hierarchy: DIKW & Decision Making

The lecture establishes the hierarchical continuum connecting raw inputs to executive action:

- **Data:** Unprocessed, objective signals and raw figures that exist without surrounding context (e.g., uncontextualized strings and numbers such as `"M"`, `"12"`, `"211092"`).
- **Information:** Data infused with contextual meaning and associative relationships (e.g., mapping `"M"` as Gender, `"12"` as Age, and `"211092"` as Birthdate).
- **Knowledge:** The synthesis of structured information with analytical frameworks, business rules, and cognitive evaluation to identify patterns and understand _why_ events occur.
- **Wisdom & Decision:** Actionable insight that anticipates future outcomes and identifies optimal principles (_what is best_), culminating in purposeful execution (_what action to take_).

In a retail analytics scenario, this flows from **transaction logs** (Data) $\rightarrow$ **sales spike correlation with rainy days** (Information) $\rightarrow$ **combining weather forecasts with promotion response rates** (Knowledge) $\rightarrow$ **anticipating customer purchasing habits during storms** (Wisdom) $\rightarrow$ **automatically dispatching umbrella discount coupons to mobile devices in rain-affected regions** (Decision).

#### 2. Typologies, Taxonomies, and Origins of Data

- **Data Provenance:** Data is segmented into **Internal vs. External** (based on whether it originates within internal corporate networks or outside environments) and **Direct vs. Indirect** (based on primary purpose of collection versus secondary analytical reuse, such as mining cellular call billing logs to determine dynamic urban population densities).
- **Statistical Classifications:**
  - _Qualitative (Categorical):_ **Nominal** (unordered sets, e.g., nationalities, professions) and **Ordinal** (ranked scales, e.g., academic ranks, performance tiers).
  - _Quantitative (Numerical):_ **Discrete** (countable integers, non-divisible, e.g., headcount) and **Continuous** (infinitely divisible real values, e.g., distances, weights).

#### 3. Data File Formats and Serialization

The technical mechanisms for storing data encompass four major categories:

- **Structured Formats:** Delimited files (CSV using commas, TSV using tab separators `\t`), spreadsheets, and **fixed-length flat files** (where columns are strictly partitioned by character offsets, explicit field widths, and strong data typing: character, numeric double, categorical, logical).
- **Semi-Structured Formats:** Self-describing, human-readable data:
  - _JSON:_ Lightweight, minimal syntax optimized for high-throughput web transmission via RESTful APIs.
  - _XML:_ Schema-enforced hierarchic markup used in enterprise SOAP architectures. It is strictly validated using **XML Schemas (XSD)**, which enforce sequences, elements, attributes, nullability, and explicit data types (`xs:string`, `xs:positiveInteger`, `xs:decimal`).
  - _YAML:_ Clean, indentation-delimited format suited for configuration.
- **Unstructured Formats:** Natural language documents, server log files, audio, video, and image binaries.
- **Big Data Analytical Formats:** Distributed systems use specialized formats including **Apache Parquet** (column-oriented storage engine for Apache Spark), **ORC** (Optimized Row Columnar, built for Hadoop and Hive performance), and **SequenceFiles** (binary key-value serializations for MapReduce).

#### 4. Enterprise Data Architecture and Management

- **Core Value Drivers:** Data Management encompasses the institutional disciplines required to acquire, organize, govern, and utilize data. It drives six operational benefits: increasing employee productivity, reducing cost overhead by eliminating duplicated research, enabling market nimbleness, minimizing security vulnerabilities, preventing catastrophic data loss via backup policies, and ensuring accurate, data-driven decisions.
- **Architectural Blueprints:** Multi-tiered data pipelines unify ingestion and consumption:
  1.  _Data Sources:_ Disparate transactional engines (OLTP, ERP, CRM), flat files, and web APIs.
  2.  _Operational Data Exchange:_ Central landing zone utilizing SQL engines or massive distributed Data Lakes for raw staging.
  3.  _Modern Data Warehouse:_ Cleaned, normalized, governed, and enriched historical tables.
  4.  _Semantic Models:_ Governed business logic layered for functional domains (Sales, Marketing, HR, Finance).
  5.  _Analytics & Consumption:_ Dashboards (Power BI, Tableau, Qlik) and advanced statistical computing (Machine Learning, predictive models).

#### 5. Data Lifecycle Management (DLM) and Cataloging

- **The DLM Framework:** Maximizes the business utility of data across five sequential phases:
  1.  _Acquisition:_ Manual or programmatic ingestion from internal/external channels.
  2.  _Storage:_ Cloud-based or on-premises persistence with automated backup policies.
  3.  _Processing:_ Data cleansing, normalization, deduplication, and transformation.
  4.  _Usage:_ Operational analytics, dashboarding, and decision-making.
  5.  _Disposal:_ Secure purging or regulatory cold archival once retention expires.
- **Data Catalogs:** Centralized metadata inventories that index corporate datasets to ensure searchability, compliance, and transparency. Datasets are tagged with governance metadata (licensing, maintainer emails, unique identifiers, spatial scopes) and update intervals structured around the **ISO 8601 Accrual Periodicity** standard (e.g., `R/P1Y` for Annual, `R/P1D` for Daily, `R/P2W` for Biweekly).

#### 6. Distributed Data Modeling Paradigms

Data models must satisfy scalability (horizontal node scaling for petabytes), flexibility (**schema-on-read** vs. **schema-on-write**), and performance:

- **Relational:** Standard tables with strict relational integrity, common in transactional systems.
- **Dimensional:** Star and snowflake schemas optimized for fast OLAP aggregations in data warehouses.
- **Document:** Hierarchical collections (MongoDB, Couchbase) mapping directly to JSON application objects.
- **Key-Value:** High-performance, low-latency hash maps (Redis, DynamoDB).
- **Column-Family:** Wide-column models (Cassandra, HBase) optimized for massive distributed writes.
- **Graph:** Edge-node graph structures (Neo4j) tracking interconnected networks and relationships.
- _Distributed Modeling Needs:_ Multi-node systems demand deliberate partition keys, data locality optimization, cross-node replication, and built-in fault tolerance.

#### 7. Data Integration, Cloud ETL, and Analytics

- **Integration Engines:** Overcome enterprise data silos through coordinated ETL pipelines. The modern stack includes tools like Informatica, Talend, Apache NiFi, Azure Data Factory, SSIS, and **AWS Glue**.
- **Serverless Ingestion (AWS Glue):** Employs automated **Glue Crawlers** to infer schema definitions from raw objects stored across S3, RDS, or Redshift, synchronizing them into the **Glue Data Catalog** using Hive DDL definitions. These catalogs are immediately queried by downstream engines like Amazon Athena, Redshift Spectrum, EMR, and QuickSight.
- **Visual Data Prep & ML Pipelines:** Tools such as **RapidMiner** operationalize workflows through directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) of operators—from ingestion (`Read CSV`) to transformation (`Select Attributes`, `Append`, `Numerical to Polynominal`, `Filter Examples`) and eventual persistence (`Write CSV`).

#### 8. Master Data Management (MDM)

MDM establishes a single, verified master data service across core corporate domains:

- **Master Data:** The foundational, persistent nouns of an organization (Customers, Products, Stores, Facilities, Employees).
- **Transaction Data:** The transient, event-driven records representing operational activity linking master entities together (e.g., an Order capturing that _Customer A_ bought _Product B_ at _Store C_ for _300 Baht_).
- **Reference Data:** Static reference sets used for categorization and conversion (e.g., lookup tables for state codes, ISO country codes, currency conversions).

#### 9. Operational Data Characteristics: Latency and Volatility

- **Latency Spectrum:**
  - _Real-time:_ Sub-second response ($\le 500\text{ ms}$).
  - _Near real-time:_ 1–2 second perceived immediacy.
  - _Delayed/Batch:_ Scheduled recurring execution (daily, weekly, monthly cycles).
- **Volatility Classifications:**
  - _Static Data:_ Append-only, chronological event records following a **Write-Once, Read-Many (WORM)** pattern (e.g., web logs, POS logs, bank debit histories, stock trading streams); grows rapidly into terabytes.
  - _Dynamic Data:_ Continually mutating, state-driven data stored in normalized RDBMS that must enforce continuous data integrity via frequent atomic read/write operations.
